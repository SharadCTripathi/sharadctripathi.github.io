Kinetic Theory of Gases and Its Applications

by Dr. Sharad Chandra Tripathi

What Is the Kinetic Theory of Gases?

Let’s explore what happens inside a gas at the molecular level. The Kinetic Theory of Gases helps us understand how a huge number of tiny particles, molecules or atoms, zoom around randomly, bump into each other, and hit the container walls. All of this chaotic motion, surprisingly, gives rise to very predictable things like pressure and temperature.

This theory acts as a bridge between the microscopic world (molecular motion) and the macroscopic quantities we measure in labs—pressure, volume, temperature, and so on.

Here are the main assumptions this theory makes:

  • Gases consist of a large number of extremely small molecules.
  • These molecules are always moving in random, straight-line paths until they collide.
  • All collisions—whether between molecules or with container walls—are perfectly elastic (no energy is lost).
  • The actual volume of gas molecules is negligible compared to the space they occupy.
  • Except during collisions, no attractive or repulsive forces act between molecules.

Of course, these assumptions are idealizations. Real gases deviate from them under high pressure or low temperature, but for most practical purposes, especially at room temperature and atmospheric pressure, this model works beautifully.

Key Concepts and Definitions

  • Pressure of a Gas:
    Caused by molecules colliding with container walls.
    \[ P = \frac{1}{3} \rho \bar{c}^2 \]
    where \( \rho \) is the mass density and \( \bar{c} \) is the root mean square speed.
  • Root Mean Square Speed (rms speed):
    \[ c_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3kT}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}} \]
    Here \( k \) is Boltzmann constant, \( T \) is temperature, \( R \) is gas constant, and \( M \) is molar mass.
  • Molecular Speed Distribution:
    Not all molecules move at the same speed. Their speeds follow the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution.
  • Collision Frequency \( (z) \):
    \[ z \propto n \bar{c} \]
    where \( n \) is number density and \( \bar{c} \) is average speed.
  • Mean Free Path \( (\lambda) \):
    The average distance a molecule travels before colliding with another molecule.
  • Kinetic Energy of One Molecule:
    \[ KE = \frac{3}{2}kT \]
  • Kinetic Energy of One Mole of Gas:
    \[ KE = \frac{3}{2}RT \]
  • Ideal Gas Equation:
    \[ PV = nRT \]
  • Degrees of Freedom:
    Independent ways a molecule can store energy: translational, rotational and vibrational.

Shortcut Concepts

  • Gas pressure = molecules hitting container walls.
  • Temperature = average kinetic energy.
  • \( KE_{\text{avg}} \propto T \)
  • Lighter gases move faster at the same temperature.
  • Specific heat relates to degrees of freedom.

Examples

  1. Calculate rms speed

    Find rms speed of oxygen molecules at \( 300\,\text{K} \). Molar mass \( M = 32\,\text{g/mol} \).

    Convert molar mass:

    \( M = 0.032\,\text{kg/mol} \)

    \[ c_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}} = \sqrt{\frac{3 \cdot 8.314 \cdot 300}{0.032}} \approx 483\,\text{m/s} \]
  2. Find pressure from rms speed

    A gas with density \( \rho = 1.5\,\text{kg/m}^3 \) has \( c_{\text{rms}} = 500\,\text{m/s} \).

    \[ P = \frac{1}{3} \rho c_{\text{rms}}^2 = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 1.5 \cdot (500)^2 = 125000\,\text{Pa} \]
  3. Average Kinetic Energy
    \[ KE = \frac{3}{2}kT = \frac{3}{2} \cdot 1.38 \times 10^{-23} \cdot 300 \approx 6.21 \times 10^{-21}\,\text{J} \]

Concept Questions with Explanations

  1. What causes gas pressure?
    Billions of molecules colliding with container walls every second.
  2. Why is temperature linked to molecular energy?
    Temperature measures average kinetic energy of molecules.
  3. Do all molecules move at the same speed?
    No. Speeds follow Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution.
  4. What is mean free path?
    Average distance traveled before collision.
  5. What happens when gas is compressed?
    Collisions increase and pressure rises.

Super Tips for Solving Fast

  • For rms speed
    \[ c_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}} \]
  • Pressure relation
    \[ P = \frac{1}{3}\rho \bar{c}^2 \]
  • Average kinetic energy
    \[ KE_{\text{avg}} = \frac{3}{2}kT \]
  • Constants
    \[ R = 8.314\,\text{J/mol·K} \quad k = 1.38 \times 10^{-23}\,\text{J/K} \]
  • Gas law relations
    \[ PV = nRT \quad \frac{P_1V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2V_2}{T_2} \]

Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

Q1.
The number of air molecules per cm3 increased from \(3 \times 10^{19}\) to \(12 \times 10^{19}\). Find ratio of collision frequencies. [JEE 2023]

  1. 0.75
  2. 0.50
  3. 1.25
  4. 0.25

Solution:

Collision frequency \( z \propto n \)

\[ \frac{z_1}{z_2} = \frac{n_1}{n_2} = \frac{3 \times 10^{19}}{12 \times 10^{19}} = \frac{1}{4} = 0.25 \]

Answer: \( \boxed{0.25} \)
