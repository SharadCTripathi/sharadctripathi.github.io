Oscillation and Simple Harmonic Motion

by Dr. Sharad Chandra Tripathi

What Is Oscillation?

Oscillation refers to the repetitive back-and-forth motion of a system about an equilibrium position. This motion occurs due to the presence of restoring forces that tend to bring the system back to its mean position whenever it is displaced.

Oscillatory motion is very common in nature. Examples include the swinging of a pendulum, vibrations of atoms in solids, oscillations of electrical currents in circuits, and vibrations of strings in musical instruments.

The defining characteristic of oscillatory motion is that the motion repeats itself after equal intervals of time. Such motion is called periodic motion.

Types of Oscillations

Oscillations can be classified into different categories depending on the physical conditions:

  • Free Oscillations: When a system oscillates under the action of restoring force alone without external forces.
  • Damped Oscillations: When resistive forces like friction cause the amplitude of oscillations to decrease with time.
  • Forced Oscillations: When an external periodic force continuously drives the system.
  • Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM): A special type of oscillation where restoring force is directly proportional to displacement.

Condition for Simple Harmonic Motion

For a motion to be classified as simple harmonic motion, the restoring force must be proportional to the displacement and directed towards the equilibrium position.

\[ F = -kx \]

Using Newton’s second law:

\[ m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = -kx \]

This differential equation describes simple harmonic motion.

Mathematical Description of SHM

The displacement of a particle executing SHM can be expressed as:

\[ x(t) = A \sin(\omega t + \phi) \]

  • \(A\) : Amplitude of oscillation
  • \( \omega \) : Angular frequency
  • \( \phi \) : Phase constant

Energy in Simple Harmonic Motion

The total mechanical energy in SHM is conserved and consists of kinetic and potential energy.

Potential energy:

\[ PE = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 \]

Kinetic energy:

\[ KE = \frac{1}{2}k(A^2-x^2) \]

Total energy:

\[ E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 \]

Key Points

  • SHM occurs when restoring force is proportional to displacement.
  • Total mechanical energy remains constant.
  • Velocity is maximum at the mean position.
  • Acceleration is maximum at extreme positions.

Examples of Oscillatory Systems

  • Mass attached to a spring.
  • Simple pendulum for small angular displacement.
  • Vibrations of atoms in crystal lattices.
  • Electrical oscillations in LC circuits.

Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

Q1. (JEE Main) A particle performs simple harmonic motion. The ratio of potential energy to kinetic energy when displacement equals half of amplitude is:

  1. 2 : 1
  2. 1 : 3
  3. 1 : 4
  4. 1 : 1

Solution:

Potential energy:

\[ PE = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 \]

Kinetic energy:

\[ KE = \frac{1}{2}k(A^2-x^2) \]

At \( x = A/2 \):

\[ \frac{PE}{KE} = \frac{(A/2)^2}{A^2-(A/2)^2} = \frac{1}{3} \]

Answer: \(1:3\)
