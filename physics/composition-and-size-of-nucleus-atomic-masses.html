Composition and Size of Nucleus, Atomic Masses

What Is the Atomic Nucleus?

The atomic nucleus is the dense central core of an atom, containing protons and neutrons, collectively known as nucleons. The nucleus carries almost the entire mass of the atom but occupies a very small volume compared to the atom’s total size.

  • Proton (p): Positively charged particle with a charge of \(+1.6 \times 10^{-19}\) C and mass \(\approx 1.007 \, \text{u}\).

  • Neutron (n): Neutral particle with no charge and mass \(\approx 1.009 \, \text{u}\).

  • Nucleons: Protons and neutrons inside the nucleus.

The number of protons defines the atomic number \(Z\), while the total number of nucleons defines the mass number \(A\). The number of neutrons \(N\) is given by: \[N = A - Z.\]

Size of the Nucleus

Nuclear sizes are extremely small, typically in the range of femtometers (1 fm = \(10^{-15}\) m). The radius of a nucleus is given approximately by the empirical formula: \[R = R_0 A^{1/3},\] where

  • \(R\): nuclear radius,

  • \(R_0 \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-15} \, \text{m}\),

  • \(A\): mass number of the nucleus.

This shows that the volume of a nucleus is roughly proportional to the number of nucleons, suggesting nearly constant nuclear density.

Atomic Mass and Mass Defect

The atomic mass of an element is the weighted average mass of its isotopes, expressed in atomic mass units (u or amu), where: \[1 \, \text{u} = \frac{1}{12} \text{mass of one atom of } \ce{^{12}C} \approx 1.66 \times 10^{-27} \, \text{kg}.\]

Mass Defect (\(\Delta m\)) is the difference between the mass of the separated nucleons and the actual mass of the nucleus: \[\Delta m = Zm_p + Nm_n - M_{\text{nucleus}}.\]

This missing mass is converted into binding energy via Einstein’s equation: \[E_b = \Delta m c^2.\]

Binding Energy and Stability

Binding energy is the energy required to disassemble a nucleus into its individual protons and neutrons. A higher binding energy per nucleon indicates greater stability of the nucleus.

  • Light nuclei: Binding energy increases with mass number.

  • Around Iron (\(A \approx 56\)): Maximum binding energy per nucleon.

  • Heavy nuclei: Binding energy per nucleon decreases, leading to fission in some cases.

Shortcut Concepts and Units

  • Atomic radius \(\sim 10^{-10}\) m, nuclear radius \(\sim 10^{-15}\) m.

  • Nuclear density is nearly constant for all nuclei: \[\rho \approx 2.3 \times 10^{17} \, \text{kg/m}^3.\]

  • 1 amu energy equivalent: \[1 \, \text{u} = 931.5 \, \text{MeV}/c^2.\]

Concept Questions with Explanations

  1. Which force holds the nucleus together?
    Answer: The strong nuclear force, acting between nucleons.

  2. Why is there a mass defect?
    Answer: Some mass converts to binding energy, holding nucleons together.

  3. Which nucleus is most stable?
    Answer: Iron-56, due to highest binding energy per nucleon.

  4. What determines nuclear size?
    Answer: Mass number \(A\) via \(R = R_0 A^{1/3}\).

Super Tips for Solving Fast

  • Use \(R = 1.2 A^{1/3} \, \text{fm}\) for quick nuclear radius estimates.

  • Remember: Binding energy per nucleon \(\approx \frac{E_b}{A}\).

  • Mass defect questions often relate to energy using \(E = \Delta m c^2\).

  • For stable nuclei, \(N \approx Z\) for light elements, \(N > Z\) for heavier ones.

Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

(NEET 2022): A nucleus of mass number 189 splits into two nuclei having mass numbers 125 and 64. The ratio of the radius of two daughter nuclei respectively is:

  1. 25:16

  2. 1:1

  3. 4:5

  4. 5:4

Solution:

Nuclear radius is given by: \[R = R_0 A^{1/3}\]

So, ratio of radii is: \[\frac{R_1}{R_2} = \left( \frac{125}{64} \right)^{1/3} = \left( \frac{5^3}{4^3} \right)^{1/3} = \frac{5}{4}\]

Answer: \(\boxed{4} \Rightarrow \boxed{5:4}\)
