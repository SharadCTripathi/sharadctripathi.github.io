Static and Kinetic Friction and Laws of Friction

by Dr. Sharad Chandra Tripathi

What Is Friction?

Friction is a fundamental force encountered in everyday life and physics. It is a contact force that resists the relative motion or the tendency of motion between two surfaces in contact. Unlike forces such as gravity or tension, friction acts tangentially along the surfaces and always opposes the direction of potential or actual sliding.

Friction plays a crucial role in many practical situations, such as walking without slipping, car tires gripping the road, and the ability to hold objects.

Types of Friction

  • Static Friction: This force acts when the object is at rest relative to the surface. It prevents the object from starting to move. Static friction adjusts itself to match the applied force up to a maximum limit.
  • Kinetic (Sliding) Friction: Once the object begins to move, static friction transitions to kinetic friction, which acts against the motion during sliding.

Laws of Friction

  1. Frictional Force is Proportional to the Normal Force
    \[ f \leq \mu_s N \]
    \[ f_k = \mu_k N \]
    where \( \mu_s \) and \( \mu_k \) are coefficients of friction.
  2. Static Friction Adjusts Up to a Maximum Maximum value \( \mu_s N \).
  3. Kinetic Friction is Constant
    \[ \mu_k < \mu_s \]
  4. Friction is Independent of Contact Area
  5. Friction Depends on Surface Nature

Key Concepts and Definitions

  • Normal Force (N): perpendicular force exerted by surface.
  • Coefficient of Static Friction \( (\mu_s) \)
  • Coefficient of Kinetic Friction \( (\mu_k) \)
  • Limiting Friction
  • Friction Direction: opposes relative motion.

Understanding Friction Through Equations

\[ f \leq \mu_s N \]
\[ f_k = \mu_k N \]

Shortcut Concepts for Quick Problem Solving

  • \[ f \leq \mu_s N \]
  • \[ mg \sin \theta = \mu_s mg \cos \theta \]
    \[ \tan \theta = \mu_s \]

Practical Examples

  1. Block on Horizontal Surface
    \[ N = mg = 10 \times 9.8 = 98\,\text{N} \]
    \[ f_{\text{max}} = \mu_s N = 0.4 \times 98 = 39.2\,\text{N} \]
    Minimum force:
    \[ \boxed{39.2\,\text{N}} \]
  2. Block on Inclined Plane
    \[ \tan \theta = \mu_s \]
    \[ \mu_s = \tan 30^\circ = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \approx \boxed{0.577} \]

Why Does Friction Occur?

Friction arises due to microscopic irregularities and asperities on the surfaces, which interlock and resist motion.

Common Misconceptions About Friction

  • Friction opposes motion, not force.
  • Static friction is not always maximum.
  • Kinetic friction is less than static friction.
  • Contact area does not affect dry friction.

Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

Q1. A block of mass \(100\,\text{kg}\) slides over \(10\,\text{m}\). Coefficient of friction \(0.4\). Find work done against friction. (JEE 2024)

  1. \(3900\,\text{J}\)
  2. \(4500\,\text{J}\)
  3. \(4200\,\text{J}\)
  4. \(4000\,\text{J}\)

Solution:

\[ N = mg = 100 \cdot 10 = 1000\,\text{N} \]
\[ f = \mu N = 0.4 \cdot 1000 = 400\,\text{N} \]
\[ W = f \cdot d = 400 \cdot 10 = \boxed{4000\,\text{J}} \]

Answer: \( \boxed{4000\,\text{J}} \)
