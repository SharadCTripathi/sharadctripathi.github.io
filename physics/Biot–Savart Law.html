Biot–Savart Law and Its Application to Circular Current Loop

What Is Biot–Savart Law?

The Biot–Savart Law describes the magnetic field produced by a small segment of current-carrying conductor. It is the magnetic analog of Coulomb’s law, applying to steady currents.

For a small current element \(I\,d\vec{l}\), the magnetic field at point \(\vec{r}\) is:

\[d\vec{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I\, d\vec{l} \times \hat{r}}{r^2}\]

Where:

  • \(\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7} \, \text{T m/A}\): Permeability of free space

  • \(I\): Current

  • \(d\vec{l}\): Element vector along current

  • \(\vec{r}\): Position vector from element to observation point

  • \(\hat{r} = \vec{r}/r\): Unit vector pointing from current element to field point

Total Magnetic Field

The total field is the vector sum (integral) of contributions over the entire current:

\[\vec{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \int \frac{I\, d\vec{l} \times \hat{r}}{r^2}\]

Magnetic Field of a Long Straight Conductor

At a perpendicular distance \(r\) from an infinitely long wire carrying current \(I\):

\[B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}\]

Direction follows the right-hand rule: curl fingers in current direction; thumb points to \(\vec{B}\).

Circular Current Loop

Consider a loop of radius \(R\) carrying current \(I\).

Field at the Center

Symmetry ensures the magnetic field at the center is:

\[B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R}\]

The direction is perpendicular to the loop’s plane (use right-hand thumb rule).

Field on the Axis

At distance \(x\) along the loop’s axis:

\[B = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2 (R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}\]

At \(x=0\), this reduces to the center value.

Key Points

  • Only the axial component of \(\vec{B}\) survives due to symmetry.

  • Right-hand rule determines the direction of \(\vec{B}\).

  • The field inside the loop (away from the axis) is non-uniform.

Examples

  1. A loop with radius \(0.05\,\text{m}\), current \(5\,\text{A}\), field at center:

    \[B = \frac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 5}{2 \times 0.05} = 10^{-5} \pi \, \text{T}\]

  2. At \(x = 0.05\,\text{m}\) on the axis:

    \[B = \frac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 5 \times (0.05)^2}{2 \left[(0.05)^2 + (0.05)^2\right]^{3/2}}\]

  3. Electron moves parallel to a long wire carrying \(5\,\text{A}\), at \(0.2\,\text{m}\):

    Magnetic field at electron:

    \[B = \frac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 5}{2\pi \times 0.2} = 5 \times 10^{-5} \, \text{T}\]

    Force on electron moving at \(105\,\text{m/s}\):

    \[F = |q| v B = (1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \times 105 \times 5 \times 10^{-5} = 8.4 \times 10^{-22} \, \text{N}\]

    Answer: \(8.4 \times 10^{-22} \, \text{N}\)

Concept Questions

  1. Why is Biot–Savart Law important?
    It allows calculation of magnetic fields from arbitrary current shapes.

  2. Direction of magnetic field at loop’s center?
    Perpendicular to loop plane by right-hand rule.

  3. How does the field vary on the axis?
    Decreases as \((R^2 + x^2)^{-3/2}\).

  4. Field at infinity on axis?
    Approaches zero as \(x \to \infty\).

  5. Is Biot–Savart Law valid for time-varying currents?
    No, only steady currents.

Super Tips

  • Memorize \(B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R}\) at center of loop.

  • On-axis field: \(B = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}\).

  • Use right-hand rule to determine field direction quickly.

  • For straight wires, use \(B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}\).

  • Use superposition for composite loops.

Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

Q1. Given below are two statements:

  • Statement I: Biot-Savart’s law gives us the expression for the magnetic field strength of an infinitesimal current element (\(I\,d\vec{l}\)) of a current-carrying conductor only.

  • Statement II: Biot-Savart’s law is analogous to Coulomb’s inverse square law of charge \(q\), with the former being related to the field produced by a scalar source, \(I\,d\vec{l}\) while the latter being produced by a vector source, \(q\).

In light of the above statements, choose the most appropriate answer from the options given below:

  1. Both Statement I and Statement II are correct

  2. Both Statement I and Statement II are incorrect

  3. Statement I is correct and Statement II is incorrect

  4. Statement I is incorrect and Statement II is correct

(NEET 2022)
Solution:

  • Statement I is correct — Biot-Savart’s law gives the magnetic field due to a small current element \(I\,d\vec{l}\), integrated over the entire conductor to get total field.

  • Statement II is incorrect — The analogy with Coulomb’s law is valid in the inverse square form, but the source nature is reversed: Biot–Savart has a vector source, Coulomb’s has scalar.

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{(c) Statement I is correct and Statement II is incorrect}}\)

Q2. An infinitely long straight conductor carries a current of \(5\,\text{A}\). An electron is moving with a speed of \(10^5\,\text{m/s}\) parallel to the conductor. The perpendicular distance between the electron and the conductor is \(20\,\text{cm}\) at an instant. Calculate the magnitude of the force experienced by the electron at that instant.
(NEET 2021)
Solution:

Step 1: Magnetic field due to long straight conductor

The magnetic field at a distance \(r\) is:

\[B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}\]

Substituting:

\[B = \frac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 5}{2\pi \times 0.2} = 5 \times 10^{-6} \, \text{T}\]

Step 2: Magnetic force on electron

Using:

\[F = |q| v B \sin\theta\]

With \(\theta = 90^\circ\):

\[F = (1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \times (10^5) \times (5 \times 10^{-6}) = 8.0 \times 10^{-20} \, \text{N}\]

Answer: \(\boxed{8.0 \times 10^{-20} \, \text{N}}\)
