What Is Biot–Savart Law?

The Biot–Savart Law is your go-to formula when you want to find the magnetic field produced by a small segment of a current-carrying conductor. Think of it as the magnetic version of Coulomb’s law, but specifically for steady currents.

For a tiny current element $I\,d\vec{l}$, the magnetic field at a point $\vec{r}$ is:

$$ d\vec{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I\, d\vec{l} \times \hat{r}}{r^2} $$

Where:

  • $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7} \, \text{T·m/A}$: permeability of free space
  • $I$: current flowing through the wire
  • $d\vec{l}$: vector along the current element
  • $\vec{r}$: position vector from the element to the observation point
  • $\hat{r} = \vec{r}/r$: unit vector pointing from the element to the observation point

Notice the cross product! This ensures the magnetic field is always perpendicular to both the current direction and the line connecting the element to the point.

Total Magnetic Field

In a real wire, there are countless tiny current elements. The total magnetic field is just the sum (integral) of all contributions along the wire:

$$ \vec{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \int \frac{I\, d\vec{l} \times \hat{r}}{r^2} $$

This formula is the foundation for calculating magnetic fields in wires, loops, coils, and solenoids.

Magnetic Field of a Long Straight Conductor

For a straight wire carrying current $I$, the magnetic field at a perpendicular distance $r$ is:

$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r} $$

To determine the direction, simply use the right-hand thumb rule: point your thumb along the current, and the curl of your fingers shows the direction of the magnetic field.

Circular Current Loop

Now let's bend the wire into a circular loop of radius $R$ carrying current $I$. Every little segment contributes to the field at the center.

Field at the Center

Due to symmetry, horizontal components cancel, leaving a field perpendicular to the plane:

$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R} $$

Field on the Axis

At a distance $x$ from the center along the axis of the loop:

$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}} $$

Substitute $x=0$ to recover the field at the center.

Key Points

  • Only the axial component of $\vec{B}$ survives due to symmetry.
  • The right-hand rule gives the direction of $\vec{B}$.
  • Inside the loop (away from axis), the field is non-uniform.

Examples

  1. Loop radius $0.05\,\text{m}$, current $5\,\text{A}$, field at center: $$ B = \frac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 5}{2 \times 0.05} = 10^{-5} \pi \, \text{T} $$
  2. At $x = 0.05\,\text{m}$ on the axis: $$ B = \frac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 5 \times (0.05)^2}{2 \left[(0.05)^2 + (0.05)^2\right]^{3/2}} $$
  3. Electron moves parallel to a wire carrying $5\,\text{A}$ at $0.2\,\text{m}$: $$ B = \frac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 5}{2\pi \times 0.2} = 5 \times 10^{-5} \, \text{T} $$ Force on electron moving at $105\,\text{m/s}$: $$ F = |q| v B = (1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \times 105 \times 5 \times 10^{-5} = 8.4 \times 10^{-22} \, \text{N} $$ Answer: $8.4 \times 10^{-22} \, \text{N}$

Concept Questions

  1. Why is Biot–Savart Law important?
    It allows calculation of magnetic fields from any current shape.
  2. Direction of magnetic field at loop’s center?
    Perpendicular to loop plane using right-hand rule.
  3. How does the field vary on the axis?
    Decreases as $(R^2 + x^2)^{-3/2}$.
  4. Field at infinity on axis?
    Approaches zero as $x \to \infty$.
  5. Is Biot–Savart Law valid for time-varying currents?
    No, only for steady currents.

Super Tips

  • Memorize $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R}$ at the center of a loop.
  • On-axis field: $B = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}$.
  • Use right-hand rule to quickly find field direction.
  • Straight wires: $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$.
  • Use superposition for composite loops.

Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

Q1. Two statements:

  • Statement I: Biot-Savart's law gives the magnetic field of an infinitesimal current element $I\,d\vec{l}$.
  • Statement II: Biot-Savart's law is analogous to Coulomb’s law of charge $q$, with source nature reversed.

Choose the correct option:

  1. Both statements are correct
  2. Both statements are incorrect
  3. Statement I correct, Statement II incorrect
  4. Statement I incorrect, Statement II correct

(NEET 2022)

Solution:

  • Statement I is correct: Integrated over entire conductor.
  • Statement II is incorrect: Analogy valid for inverse-square, source is vector vs scalar.

Answer: $\boxed{\text{(c) Statement I is correct and Statement II is incorrect}}$

Q2. Electron moving parallel to long straight wire carrying $5\,\text{A}$ at $0.2\,\text{m}$, speed $10^5\,\text{m/s}$.

(NEET 2021)

Solution:

Step 1: Magnetic field at distance $r$:

$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r} = \frac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 5}{2\pi \times 0.2} = 5 \times 10^{-6} \, \text{T} $$

Step 2: Magnetic force on electron ($\theta=90^\circ$):

$$ F = |q| v B \sin\theta = (1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \times 10^5 \times 5 \times 10^{-6} = 8.0 \times 10^{-20} \, \text{N} $$

Answer: $\boxed{8.0 \times 10^{-20} \, \text{N}}$
