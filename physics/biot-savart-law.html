Biot–Savart Law and Its Application to Circular Current Loop

Understanding magnetic fields produced by currents made simple!

What Is Biot–Savart Law?

The Biot–Savart Law describes the magnetic field produced by a small segment of a current-carrying conductor. Think of it as the magnetic version of Coulomb’s law — but for steady currents!

For a small current element \(I\,d\vec{l}\), the magnetic field at point \(\vec{r}\) is:

\(d\vec{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I\, d\vec{l} \times \hat{r}}{r^2}\)

Where:

  • \(\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7} \, \text{T m/A}\): Permeability of free space
  • I: Current
  • d\vec{l}: Element vector along current
  • \(\vec{r}\): Position vector from element to observation point
  • \(\hat{r} = \vec{r}/r\): Unit vector pointing from current element to field point

Total Magnetic Field

The total magnetic field is the vector sum of contributions from every element of the current:

\(\vec{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \int \frac{I\, d\vec{l} \times \hat{r}}{r^2}\)

Magnetic Field of a Long Straight Conductor

At a perpendicular distance r from an infinitely long wire carrying current I:

B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}

Direction? Use the right-hand rule: curl your fingers along current, thumb points along \(\vec{B}\).

Circular Current Loop

Consider a loop of radius R carrying current I.

Field at the Center

By symmetry, the magnetic field at the center is:

B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R}

Direction: perpendicular to loop plane (use right-hand thumb rule).

Field on the Axis

At a distance x along the loop’s axis:

B = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2 (R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}

At x=0, this reduces to the center field.

Key Points

  • Only the axial component of \(\vec{B}\) survives due to symmetry.
  • Right-hand rule quickly determines direction.
  • Field inside loop (off-axis) is non-uniform.

Examples

Example 1

Loop radius 0.05\,\text{m}, current 5\,\text{A}, field at center:

B = \frac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 5}{2 \times 0.05} = 10^{-5}\pi \, \text{T}

Example 2

At x = 0.05\,\text{m} on the axis:

B = \frac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 5 \times (0.05)^2}{2[(0.05)^2 + (0.05)^2]^{3/2}}

Example 3

Electron moves parallel to long wire carrying 5\,\text{A}, at 0.2\,\text{m}:

Magnetic field at electron:

B = \frac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 5}{2\pi \times 0.2} = 5 \times 10^{-5} \, \text{T}

Force on electron at speed 105\,\text{m/s}:

F = |q| v B = (1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \times 105 \times 5 \times 10^{-5} = 8.4 \times 10^{-22} \, \text{N}

Concept Questions

  1. Why is Biot–Savart Law important?
    It allows calculation of magnetic fields from arbitrary current shapes.
  2. Direction of magnetic field at loop’s center?
    Perpendicular to loop plane using right-hand rule.
  3. How does the field vary on the axis?
    Decreases as (R^2 + x^2)^{-3/2}.
  4. Field at infinity along axis?
    Approaches zero as x \to \infty.
  5. Is Biot–Savart Law valid for time-varying currents?
    No, only steady currents.

Super Tips

  • Memorize B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R} at the center of a loop.
  • On-axis field: B = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}
  • Use right-hand rule to quickly determine direction.
  • For straight wires, use B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}.
  • Use superposition for composite loops.

Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

Q1 (NEET 2022)

Two statements:

  • Statement I: Biot-Savart's law gives the magnetic field due to an infinitesimal current element (I\,d\vec{l}).
  • Statement II: Biot-Savart's law is analogous to Coulomb’s inverse square law of charge q, but the source nature differs.

Solution:

  • Statement I is correct — integrate I\,d\vec{l} over the conductor to get total field.
  • Statement II is incorrect — Coulomb has scalar source, Biot–Savart has vector source.

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{(c) Statement I is correct and Statement II is incorrect}}\)

Q2 (NEET 2021)

Long straight conductor with current 5\,\text{A}. Electron moves parallel at 10^5\,\text{m/s}, perpendicular distance 0.2\,\text{m}. Find force.

Step 1: Magnetic field:

B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r} = \frac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 5}{2\pi \times 0.2} = 5 \times 10^{-6} \, \text{T}

Step 2: Force on electron:

F = |q| v B \sin 90^\circ = (1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \times 10^5 \times 5 \times 10^{-6} = 8.0 \times 10^{-20} \, \text{N}

Answer: \(\boxed{8.0 \times 10^{-20} \, \text{N}}\)
