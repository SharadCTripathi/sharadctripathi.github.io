Photoelectric Effect and Its Applications

What Is Photoelectric Effect?

The Photoelectric Effect refers to the emission of electrons from a metal surface when it is exposed to electromagnetic radiation of sufficient frequency. It demonstrates the particle nature of light.

Key Concept: Only photons with energy greater than the material’s work function can eject electrons.

Einstein’s Photoelectric Equation

Einstein explained the effect using quantum theory. He proposed:

\[E_{\text{photon}} = h\nu = \phi + K_{\text{max}}\]

Where:

  • \(h\): Planck’s constant (\(6.626 \times 10^{-34} \, \text{J·s}\))

  • \(\nu\): Frequency of incident light

  • \(\phi\): Work function of the metal (minimum energy to eject electron)

  • \(K_{\text{max}}\): Maximum kinetic energy of ejected electrons

Threshold Frequency and Work Function

  • Threshold frequency \(\nu_0\): Minimum frequency required to emit electrons.

  • At \(\nu = \nu_0\), \(K_{\text{max}} = 0\), hence \(\phi = h\nu_0\).

Kinetic Energy and Stopping Potential

Kinetic energy is related to stopping potential \(V_0\):

\[K_{\text{max}} = e V_0\]

So, Einstein’s equation becomes:

\[h\nu = \phi + eV_0\]

Where \(e\) is the elementary charge (\(1.6 \times 10^{-19} \, \text{C}\)).

Key Points

  • Increasing intensity increases number of photoelectrons, not their energy.

  • Increasing frequency increases energy of photoelectrons.

  • No electrons are emitted if \(\nu < \nu_0\), regardless of intensity.

  • Instantaneous emission — no time lag.

  • Energy of each photon: \(E = h\nu\)

Examples

  1. If light of frequency \(\nu = 8 \times 10^{14} \, \text{Hz}\) falls on metal with \(\phi = 2 \, \text{eV}\), find \(K_{\text{max}}\).

    \[E = h\nu = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 8 \times 10^{14} = 5.3008 \times 10^{-19} \, \text{J}\]

    \[\Rightarrow E = \frac{5.3008 \times 10^{-19}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}} = 3.31 \, \text{eV}\]

    \[K_{\text{max}} = E - \phi = 3.31 - 2 = 1.31 \, \text{eV}\]

    Answer: \(1.31 \, \text{eV}\)

  2. If \(K_{\text{max}} = 1.5 \, \text{eV}\) and \(\phi = 2.5 \, \text{eV}\), find frequency of light.

    \[E = \phi + K_{\text{max}} = 4.0 \, \text{eV}\]

    \[\nu = \frac{E}{h} = \frac{4 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}}{6.626 \times 10^{-34}} \approx 9.66 \times 10^{14} \, \text{Hz}\]

    Answer: \(\approx 9.66 \times 10^{14} \, \text{Hz}\)

Concept Questions

  1. What is the effect of increasing light intensity below threshold frequency?
    No photoelectric emission occurs.

  2. Why is there no time lag in photoelectric emission?
    Energy is delivered in discrete photons, not continuously.

  3. What determines the energy of photoelectrons?
    Frequency of incident light.

  4. Can light of any frequency cause photoemission if intensity is high enough?
    No. Frequency must be above threshold.

  5. What is the nature of light suggested by the photoelectric effect?
    Particle (quantum) nature.

Super Tips

  • Remember: \(K_{\text{max}} = h\nu - \phi\)

  • \(K_{\text{max}} = eV_0\) helps determine stopping potential.

  • Photoelectric effect confirms quantum theory of light.

  • Use \(\phi = h\nu_0\) to find threshold frequency.

  • Photon energy is independent of light intensity.

Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

Q1. Light of frequency 1.5 times the threshold frequency is incident on a photosensitive material. What will be the photoelectric current if the frequency is halved and intensity is doubled?
(NEET 2020)

  1. doubled

  2. four times

  3. one-fourth

  4. zero

Solution:

Step 1: Understand initial situation Initial frequency: \(\nu = 1.5 \nu_0\), where \(\nu_0\) is the threshold frequency. Since \(\nu > \nu_0\), photoemission occurs.

Step 2: After halving frequency: New frequency: \(\nu = 0.75 \nu_0\) Since this is less than the threshold, no photoemission occurs, regardless of intensity.

Step 3: Conclusion Photoelectric current becomes zero because photons no longer have sufficient energy to eject electrons.

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{D) zero}}\)

Q2. When the energy of the incident radiation is increased by 20%, the kinetic energy of the photoelectrons emitted from a metal surface increases from \(0.5\,\text{eV}\) to \(0.8\,\text{eV}\). The work function of the metal is:
(NEET 2014)

  1. 0.65 eV

  2. 1.0 eV

  3. 1.3 eV

  4. 1.5 eV

Solution:

Let initial photon energy be \(E\), and final photon energy be \(1.2E\). From Einstein’s photoelectric equation:

\[K_1 = E - \phi \quad \text{(Initial)}\] \[K_2 = 1.2E - \phi \quad \text{(Final)}\]

Given: \[K_1 = 0.5 \, \text{eV}, \quad K_2 = 0.8 \, \text{eV}\]

Subtract the first equation from the second:

\[K_2 - K_1 = (1.2E - \phi) - (E - \phi) = 0.2E\] \[0.8 - 0.5 = 0.2E \Rightarrow 0.3 = 0.2E \Rightarrow E = \frac{0.3}{0.2} = 1.5 \, \text{eV}\]

Now substitute \(E = 1.5 \, \text{eV}\) into \(K_1 = E - \phi\):

\[0.5 = 1.5 - \phi \Rightarrow \phi = 1.0 \, \text{eV}\]

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{B) 1.0 eV}}\)
