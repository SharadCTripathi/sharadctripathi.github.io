Specific Heat Capacity

Word Count: ≈ 938

Why does water take longer to heat than metal? Why does sand get hot quickly on a sunny day but cools down just as fast at night? The answer lies in a property called specific heat capacity. It tells us how much heat is required to raise the temperature of a substance and helps explain the thermal behavior of everything from oceans to engine blocks.

Let us understand this concept in depth.

What is Specific Heat Capacity?

Specific heat capacity is defined as the amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of one gram of a substance by one degree Celsius (or one kelvin).

Mathematically, it is expressed as:

\[ Q = mc\Delta T \]

Where:

  • \( Q \) = heat supplied (in joules)
  • \( m \) = mass of the substance (in grams or kilograms)
  • \( c \) = specific heat capacity of the substance
  • \( \Delta T \) = change in temperature

SI unit: \( \text{J/kg} \cdot \text{K} \)
CGS unit: \( \text{cal/g}^\circ \text{C} \)

Note: 1 calorie = 4.186 joules

Heat Capacity vs Specific Heat Capacity

Heat Capacity (C): It is the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of the entire object by 1°C.

\[ C = mc \]

So, specific heat capacity is the heat capacity per unit mass.

Water Has High Specific Heat

Water has a very high specific heat capacity, around \( 4186 \, \text{J/kg} \cdot \text{K} \).

  • Oceans regulate Earth’s temperature.
  • Water-based coolants are used in engines.
  • Human body resists sudden temperature changes.

Understanding Through an Example

Suppose you supply 4200 J of heat to 1 kg of water. The temperature rise is:

\[ \Delta T = \frac{Q}{mc} = \frac{4200}{1 \times 4200} = 1^\circ \text{C} \]

Molar Specific Heat

Heat capacity can also be expressed per mole:

\[ C_{\text{molar}} = \frac{Q}{n \Delta T} \]

Types of Specific Heat

  • Constant volume: \( C_V \)
  • Constant pressure: \( C_P \)
\[ C_P - C_V = R \]

Calorimetry and Heat Exchange

\[ \text{Heat lost by hot body} = \text{Heat gained by cold body} \]
\[ m_1 c_1 (T_1 - T_f) = m_2 c_2 (T_f - T_2) \]

Mechanical Work and Heat

Kinetic energy:

\[ E = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \]
\[ Q = mc\Delta T \]

Previous Year Question (PYQ)

Q1. A 100 g iron nail is hit by a 1.5 kg hammer striking at velocity \(60\,\text{ms}^{-1}\). One-fourth of hammer energy heats the nail.

Specific heat of iron \(0.42\,\text{Jg}^{-1}{}^\circ\text{C}^{-1}\)

Step 1: Kinetic Energy

\[ E = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}\times1.5\times60^2 = 2700\,J \]

Step 2: Heat transferred

\[ Q = \frac{1}{4}\times2700 = 675\,J \]

Step 3: Temperature rise

\[ \Delta T=\frac{Q}{mc}=\frac{675}{100\times0.42}\approx16.07^\circ C \]

Answer: (c) \(16.07^\circ C\)
