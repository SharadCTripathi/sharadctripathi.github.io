What Is the Law of Conservation of Linear Momentum?

The Law of Conservation of Linear Momentum is a fundamental principle of physics that arises from Newton’s Second Law:

\[ \vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} \]

where \( \vec{F}_{\text{net}} \) is the net external force on a system, and \( \vec{p} = m \vec{v} \) is the linear momentum.

If the net external force acting on a system is zero:

\[ \vec{F}_{\text{net}} = 0 \]

then:

\[ \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} = 0 \Rightarrow \vec{p} = \text{constant} \]

This means the total linear momentum of an isolated system remains conserved over time. It holds for collisions, explosions, and other interactions in closed systems.

\[ m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 \]

  • \( m_1, m_2 \): Masses of the objects
  • \( u_1, u_2 \): Initial velocities
  • \( v_1, v_2 \): Final velocities

Key Concepts and Definitions

  • Linear Momentum (\( \vec{p} \)):

    \[ \vec{p} = m \vec{v} \]

    Vector quantity representing motion, in the direction of velocity.
  • Isolated System: A system not influenced by any external net force.
  • Impulse:

    \[ \text{Impulse} = F \cdot \Delta t = \Delta p \]

    Product of force and time equals change in momentum.
  • Elastic Collision: Momentum and kinetic energy conserved.
  • Inelastic Collision: Only momentum conserved; kinetic energy partially lost.
  • Perfectly Inelastic Collision: Maximum kinetic energy lost; objects stick together.

Shortcut Concepts

  • Momentum is conserved only when \( \vec{F}_{\text{ext}} = 0 \).
  • Internal forces do not affect total momentum.
  • Total momentum before and after interaction is the same in an isolated system.
  • In explosions, initial momentum is often zero if system starts at rest.
  • Direction is crucial: assign appropriate signs to velocities.

Examples

  1. Recoil of a Gun: Gun mass 5 kg fires a 0.05 kg bullet at 400 m/s. Recoil velocity of gun?

    2. \[ 0 = m_{\text{bullet}} v_{\text{bullet}} + m_{\text{gun}} v_{\text{gun}} \Rightarrow v = -4 \, \text{m/s} \]

    Negative indicates recoil opposite to bullet direction.

  2. Perfectly Inelastic Collision: 3 kg cart at 4 m/s collides with 2 kg cart at -3 m/s, stick together.

    3. \[ v = \frac{3\cdot 4 + 2 \cdot (-3)}{3+2} = \frac{6}{5} = 1.2 \, \text{m/s} \]

Concept Questions with Explanations

  1. Why is momentum conserved in absence of external forces?
    Because \( \vec{F}_{\text{ext}} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} \). If zero, \( \vec{p} \) is constant.
  2. Role of direction in momentum?
    Momentum is a vector; opposite directions imply opposite signs.
  3. Does momentum conservation apply in all collisions?
    Yes, if no external forces; kinetic energy may or may not be conserved.
  4. Momentum of a system at rest?
    Zero. After internal action, total momentum remains zero.
  5. When is impulse useful?
    When force is not constant; relates to change in momentum.

Super Tips for Solving Fast

  • Use momentum conservation:

    \[ m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 \]

  • Perfectly inelastic collision:

    \[ v = \frac{m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2}{m_1 + m_2} \]

  • Use signs for direction: right positive, left negative.
  • Explosion problems: momentum before = 0 if system at rest.
  • For 2D problems, break momentum into x & y components.

Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

Q1. Artillery piece mass \( M_1 \) fires shell mass \( M_2 \) horizontally. Ratio of kinetic energies after firing? (JEE 2024)

  1. \( \frac{M_1}{M_1+M_2} \)
  2. \( \frac{M_2}{M_1} \)
  3. \( \frac{M_1}{M_2} \)
  4. \( \frac{M_2}{M_1+M_2} \)

Solution:

  • Mass of artillery = \( M_1 \)
  • Mass of shell = \( M_2 \)
  • Initially, system at rest. After firing, they move in opposite directions.

Let velocity of shell = \( v \), artillery recoil = \( V \). Using conservation of momentum:

\[ M_1 (-V) + M_2 v = 0 \Rightarrow V = \frac{M_2}{M_1} v \]

Kinetic energies:

\[ KE_1 = \frac{1}{2} M_1 V^2 = \frac{1}{2} \frac{M_2^2}{M_1} v^2, \quad KE_2 = \frac{1}{2} M_2 v^2 \]

Ratio:

\[ \frac{KE_1}{KE_2} = \frac{M_2}{M_1} \]

Answer: \( \boxed{M_2/M_1} \)
