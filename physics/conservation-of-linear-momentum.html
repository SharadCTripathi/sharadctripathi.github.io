Law of Conservation of Linear Momentum and Its Applications

by Dr. Sharad Chandra Tripathi

What Is the Law of Conservation of Linear Momentum?

The Law of Conservation of Linear Momentum is a fundamental principle of physics that arises from Newton’s Second Law:

\[ \vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} \]

where \( \vec{F}_{\text{net}} \) is the net external force on a system, and \( \vec{p} = m \vec{v} \) is the linear momentum.

If the net external force acting on a system is zero:

\[ \vec{F}_{\text{net}} = 0 \]

then:

\[ \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} = 0 \Rightarrow \vec{p} = \text{constant} \]

This means the total linear momentum of an isolated system remains conserved over time. It holds for collisions, explosions, and other interactions in closed systems.

\[ m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 \]

  • \( m_1, m_2 \): Masses of the objects
  • \( u_1, u_2 \): Initial velocities
  • \( v_1, v_2 \): Final velocities
