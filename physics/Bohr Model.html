Bohr Model and Its Applications

What Is Bohr Model?

The Bohr Model describes the hydrogen atom by combining classical mechanics with quantum ideas. It explains the discrete spectral lines observed in atomic emission.

Key Concept: Electrons revolve in specific orbits without radiating energy and transition between these levels by absorbing or emitting photons.

Postulates of Bohr’s Model

  • Electrons orbit the nucleus in stable, quantized paths.

  • Angular momentum is quantized: \[mvr = \frac{nh}{2\pi}\]

  • Radiation is emitted/absorbed only during transitions: \[E = h\nu = E_i - E_f\]

Energy Levels in Hydrogen Atom

\[E_n = -\frac{13.6}{n^2} \, \text{eV}\]

Where \(n\) is the principal quantum number. Negative energy shows that the electron is bound.

Radius of orbit: \(r_n = n^2 a_0\), where \(a_0 = 0.529 \, \text{Å}\)

Spectral Lines

Electron transitions lead to emission or absorption of light:

\[\frac{1}{\lambda} = R_H \left( \frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2} \right)\]

Hydrogen Series:

  • Lyman (\(n_1 = 1\)): UV

  • Balmer (\(n_1 = 2\)): Visible

  • Paschen (\(n_1 = 3\)): IR

Key Points

  • Energy is quantized; levels are negative.

  • \(E_n \propto \frac{1}{n^2}\), \(r_n \propto n^2\)

  • Only hydrogen or hydrogen-like ions fit the model.

  • Photon frequency depends on energy gap.

Example

Find the energy needed to ionize a hydrogen atom from its ground state.

\[E_1 = -13.6 \, \text{eV}, \quad E_\infty = 0 \Rightarrow \text{Ionization energy} = 0 - (-13.6) = 13.6 \, \text{eV}\]

Answer: \(13.6 \, \text{eV}\)

Concept Questions

  1. Why are energy levels negative?
    The electron is bound to the nucleus.

  2. What happens when an electron absorbs energy?
    It jumps to a higher orbit (excitation).

  3. Can Bohr’s model explain multi-electron atoms?
    No, only single-electron systems.

  4. How does radius change with \(n\)?
    Increases as \(n^2\).

  5. What does Bohr’s model explain?
    Atomic spectra of hydrogen.

Super Tips

  • Energy formula: \(E_n = -13.6/n^2 \, \text{eV}\). Lower \(n\) means more tightly bound electron.

  • Radius formula: \(r_n = n^2 a_0\). Orbit size increases rapidly with \(n\); electron gets farther from nucleus.

  • Photon emission: Occurs when an electron falls from a higher to a lower level. Use: \[h\nu = E_i - E_f\]

  • Ionization energy: From ground state, it’s \(13.6 \, \text{eV}\). It’s the energy needed to move electron to \(n = \infty\).

  • Transition shortcuts: - Maximum energy: \(n = 2 \to 1\) - Visible light: Balmer series - UV: Lyman series

  • Rydberg formula tip: For hydrogen spectrum: \[\frac{1}{\lambda} = R_H \left( \frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2} \right)\] Use it to find wavelength of emitted/absorbed light.

  • Electron speed: Decreases with increasing \(n\): \(v_n \propto \frac{1}{n}\)

  • Model limitation: Bohr model works only for single-electron atoms like H, He\(^+\), Li\(^{2+}\).

Previous Year Question (PYQ)

Q1. A particle of mass \(m\) is moving around the origin with a constant force \(F\) pulling it towards the origin. If Bohr model is used to describe its motion, the radius of the \(n^\text{th}\) orbit and the particle’s speed \(v\) in the orbit depend on \(n\) as:

(NEET 2025)

  1. \(r \propto n^{2/3},\quad v \propto n^{1/3}\)

  2. \(r \propto n^{4/3},\quad v \propto n^{-1/3}\)

  3. \(r \propto n^{1/3},\quad v \propto n^{1/3}\)

  4. \(r \propto n^{1/3},\quad v \propto n^{2/3}\)

Solution:

Step 1: Use central force model Given: A constant central force \(F\) is pulling the particle towards the origin. So, centripetal force \(\frac{mv^2}{r} = F \Rightarrow v^2 \propto r\) \[\Rightarrow v \propto \sqrt{r} \tag{1}\]

Step 2: Apply Bohr’s quantization condition Angular momentum quantization: \[mvr = n\hbar \Rightarrow v \propto \frac{n}{r} \tag{2}\]

Substitute (1) into (2): \[\sqrt{r} \propto \frac{n}{r} \Rightarrow r^{3/2} \propto n \Rightarrow r \propto n^{2/3}\]

Now from (1): \(v \propto \sqrt{r} \Rightarrow v \propto n^{1/3}\)

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{A) } r \propto n^{2/3},\ v \propto n^{1/3}}\)
