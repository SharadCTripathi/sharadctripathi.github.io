What Is Wave-Particle Duality?

Wave-particle duality is a fundamental idea from early 20th-century physics, showing that microscopic entities like light and electrons behave both as particles and waves. Light, once thought purely a wave due to interference patterns, was shown by Einstein’s 1905 explanation of the photoelectric effect to have particle-like behavior, emitting energy in discrete packets called photons.

In 1924, Louis de Broglie suggested that since light has both wave and particle nature, matter particles like electrons should also have wave properties. This was later confirmed by experiments such as Davisson-Germer, which observed electron diffraction—an evidence of wave behavior.

This duality means particles have an associated wavelength, called the de Broglie wavelength, given by

\[ \lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mv}, \]

where \(h\) is Planck’s constant, \(m\) is mass, and \(v\) is velocity. Understanding this duality is key to quantum mechanics and atomic physics.

de Broglie’s Formula (Core Concept)

\[ \lambda = \frac{h}{mv} \]

Where:

  • \( \lambda \): de Broglie wavelength
  • \( h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \, \text{J·s} \)
  • \( m \): particle mass
  • \( v \): particle velocity

Heavier or faster particles have shorter wavelengths.

Shortcut Formulas for Different Cases

  • With velocity \( v \): \( \lambda = \frac{h}{mv} \)
  • With momentum \( p \): \( \lambda = \frac{h}{p} \)
  • With kinetic energy \( K \): \( \lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2mK}} \)
  • Electron accelerated by potential \( V \): \( \lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2meV}} \)
  • Electron shortcut in Ångströms: \( \lambda(\text{\AA}) \approx \frac{12.27}{\sqrt{V}} \)

Examples

  1. Electron accelerated by 150 V:

    \[ \lambda = \frac{12.27}{\sqrt{150}} \approx 1.0 \, \text{\AA} \]

  2. Electron vs proton at same kinetic energy: Electron wavelength is larger due to smaller mass.
  3. KE increases 4 times:

    \[ \lambda \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{K}} \Rightarrow \lambda' = \frac{\lambda}{2} \]

  4. Speed doubles:

    \[ \lambda \propto \frac{1}{v} \Rightarrow \lambda' = \frac{\lambda}{2} \]

Concept Questions with Explanations

  1. If KE becomes 16 times, wavelength becomes \( \frac{1}{4} \) because \( \lambda \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{K}} \).
  2. Which experiment proves wave nature of electrons?
    Answer: Davisson-Germer experiment (electron diffraction confirms wave behavior).
  3. Which experiment proves particle nature of light?
    Answer: Photoelectric effect (light ejects electrons as particles).
  4. At same KE, who has larger \( \lambda \) — electron or proton?
    Answer: Electron, since lighter mass means larger wavelength.
  5. Wavelength of electron with 100 eV energy:

    \[ \lambda \approx \frac{12.27}{\sqrt{100}} = 1.227 \, \text{\AA} \]

Super Tips for Solving Fast

  • Use \( \lambda = \frac{12.27}{\sqrt{V}} \) (Å) for electrons accelerated by voltage \( V \).
  • Remember lighter particles have larger wavelengths.
  • Convert voltage \( V \) to kinetic energy \( K = eV \).
  • Keep units consistent (SI units preferred).

Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

(JEE 2025 Mains)

An electron is released from rest near an infinite non-conducting sheet of uniform charge density \(\sigma\). The rate of change of de-Broglie wavelength associated with the electron varies inversely as the \(n^{th}\) power of time. The numerical value of \(n\) is ______.

Solution:

Step 1: The electric field near the sheet is

\[ E = \frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0}. \]

Step 2: Electron acceleration:

\[ a = \frac{eE}{m} = \frac{e\sigma}{2m\epsilon_0}. \]

Step 3: Velocity after time \( t \):

\[ v = at = \frac{e\sigma}{2m\epsilon_0} t. \]

Step 4: de Broglie wavelength:

\[ \lambda = \frac{h}{mv} = \frac{h}{m a t} \propto \frac{1}{t}. \]

Step 5: Rate of change of wavelength:

\[ \frac{d\lambda}{dt} \propto \frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{1}{t} \right) = -\frac{1}{t^2}. \]

Hence, the rate varies inversely as \(t^2\), so \(n=2\).

Answer: \( \boxed{2} \)
